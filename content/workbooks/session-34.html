Session 34: Social Media & Misinformation

TikTok, YouTube, Instagram, Facebook, Snapchat and WhatsApp channels: millions of Ghanaians scroll through them every day. In Session 33 you learnt to protect your personal information and to T.H.I.N.K. before you post. Today you will look behind the screen: how an app decides what to show you, why fake news spreads so fast, how to check a viral post, what to do about cyberbullying, and how to keep social media healthy and fun. You don't need a social media account for any of it.

1. Plain Talk: The Seller Who Watches You

Imagine a clever kenkey seller at the lorry station. She notices that every time you pass, you slow down at the fried fish. The next day she puts fried fish right at the front of her table, where you will see it first. Soon you only ever see fried fish, even though she sells many other things.

A social media feed (the endless list of posts and videos you scroll through) works the same way. A computer program called an algorithm watches what you do, and keeps putting more of the same at the front of your feed.

Figure 34.0: The feed loop. Every action you take teaches the algorithm what to show you next.
🔗 YOU WILL MEET THIS AGAIN IN SESSION 39
The feed is an AI that predicts: it learns from examples of what you watched, and guesses what will keep you scrolling. In Session 39 you will train an AI yourself and see how it learns patterns from examples.
What the Algorithm Notices & Echo Chambers
Session 34 • Page 2

1. The Clues the App Collects

The algorithm doesn't read your mind. It watches signals: small clues about what you like. The strongest signal is often simply how long you watch.

SignalWhat it tells the app
⏱ Watch timeYou watched the whole video, or watched it twice: "Show more like this."
❤️ Likes and commentsYou reacted to it: it matters to you.
↗️ SharesYou sent it to friends: it is worth spreading.
👤 FollowsYou want more from this person or page.
⏭ Scrolling past quicklyNot interested: show less of it.
🔍 SearchesWhat you looked for, and sometimes where you are and what time it is.
⚠️ WHY APPS WANT YOU TO KEEP SCROLLING
Most social media apps are free because they earn money from adverts. The longer you scroll, the more adverts you see. So the algorithm's job is to keep you watching, not to show you what is true or what is good for you.

2. Echo Chambers: Stuck in a Bubble

If you only ever see one kind of post, your feed becomes an echo chamber: a room where you only hear your own voice echoing back. You might start to believe that everyone thinks the same, or that a rumour must be true because you have seen it ten times.

Figure 34.1: An echo chamber (left) versus a healthy, varied feed (right).
💡 HOW TO POP THE BUBBLE
Follow a mix of people and topics. Tap "Not interested" on things you don't want. Search for the other side of a story. Get news from trusted news organisations too, not only from your feed.

Fake News & the W.A.I.T. Check
Session 34 • Page 3

1. Why Fake News Spreads So Fast

Misinformation is false information shared by people who think it is true. Disinformation is false information spread on purpose, to trick people or make money. Both spread fast for two reasons: emotion (shocking, scary or angry posts make us share without thinking) and speed (one tap sends it to a whole group).

Figure 34.2: How one post becomes viral. Each forward doubles the number of people who see it.

2. Five Kinds of Fake

Made up (a story that never happened) · Old photo, new story (a real flood picture from years ago, shared as "today") · Edited (a photo or screenshot changed) · Deepfake (a video or voice made by AI, as in Session 40) · Fake account (a page pretending to be a famous person, school or news station).

3. Before You Share, W.A.I.T.

LetterAsk yourself…
W – Who posted it?A real news organisation or official account, or a page you have never heard of? Is the account new? Does the name look slightly wrong?
A – Anyone else reporting it?Search for it. If it were true, would trusted news websites or the official organisation be saying it too?
I – Image checkDo a reverse image search (Session 12) to see if the picture is old or from somewhere else. Look for deepfake signs: odd hands, blurry edges, lips not matching the words.
T – Time and dateWhen was it posted? Is it an old story being shared again? Does it say "share before they delete it!"? That is pressure, not proof.
✋ IF YOU ARE NOT SURE, DON'T SHARE
You never have to be first. If a post fails W.A.I.T., don't forward it, and if it is in a group, kindly say "I checked: this isn't true" and share the link that proves it.
Cyberbullying, Privacy & Healthy Use
Session 34 • Page 4

1. Cyberbullying: What It Is and What To Do

Cyberbullying is using phones or the internet to hurt, scare or embarrass someone again and again: cruel messages, spreading rumours, sharing embarrassing photos, leaving someone out of a group on purpose, or pretending to be them. It is never the victim's fault.

Figure 34.3: Five steps if you, or someone you know, is being bullied online.

Be an upstander, not a bystander. If you see someone being bullied: don't like or share the cruel post, send the person a kind private message ("Are you okay? That wasn't fair"), report the post, and tell an adult.

2. Privacy Settings and Age Limits

🔐 Four settings to check (when you are old enough)

• Private account: only people you approve see your posts.
• Who can message me: friends only.
• Location: off, and don't tag where you are.
• Tags: approve posts others tag you in.

🎂 Why 13+?

Most platforms (TikTok, Instagram, Facebook, Snapchat, and YouTube for your own account) say you must be at least 13, and some features are only for older users. The limits exist to protect children's data and safety. Lying about your age removes that protection.

3. Healthy Use: You Are in Charge, Not the App

HabitWhy it helps
⏰ Set a time limitUse Digital Wellbeing (Android) or Screen Time (iPhone) to see your hours and set limits.
😴 Screens off an hour before bedBright screens and exciting videos keep your brain awake. Sleep helps you learn.
🪞 Remember the highlight reelPeople post their best moments, often with filters. Comparing your whole life with their best day isn't fair to you.
🔕 Turn off notificationsFewer buzzes, fewer reasons to pick up the phone during homework.
