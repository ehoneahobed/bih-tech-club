Session 14: Digital Collaboration & Teamwork

Until today, when you worked on a document, you sat alone at one computer. But in real technology companies and modern universities, projects are built by teams working simultaneously across different computers, classrooms, and even different countries!

1. Plain Talk: The African Tradition of "Nnoboa"

In Ghanaian farming communities, there is an ancient tradition called Nnoboa (communal labor). When it is time to clear a field or harvest cocoa, neighbors gather to work together. One person clears weeds, another carries water, another harvests pods. Together, they finish in one afternoon what would take one person a month!

Digital Collaboration is Nnoboa using technology. Instead of passing a single USB flash drive back and forth, teammates connect to a single document stored securely online in Cloud Storage (like Google Drive or Microsoft OneDrive) and edit together in real time!

Figure 14.0: Cloud Collaboration. Both students edit the exact same document simultaneously without USB pen drives!

2. Essential Collaboration Vocabulary

  • Cloud Storage: Safe digital space on remote internet servers (Google Drive, OneDrive) used to store and open files from any computer.
  • Shared File: A file set up so more than one approved person can open and work on it.
  • Version History: An automatic time-stamped digital memory log showing every single change made and who made it.
  • Comment: A sticky-note note pinned to the margin of a document to suggest an improvement without deleting text.
Managing Sharing Permissions
Session 14 • Page 2

1. The Three Levels of File Access

When you share a file online with classmates or teachers, you must choose their exact Permission Tier. Giving the wrong permission can let someone accidentally erase your whole project!

Figure 14.1: The Three Permission Tiers. Always give the lowest permission the person needs.

2. The Cardinal Sharing Law

🚫 NEVER SHARE AS "EDITOR" TO THE PUBLIC!
If you create a public link with Editor permission, anyone on the internet who finds the link can delete all your paragraphs, insert inappropriate pictures, or ruin your hard work.

Safety Rule: Give Editor permission ONLY to your specific, named team members. Everyone else gets Viewer or Commenter!

The Art of Constructive Feedback
Session 14 • Page 4

1. Comments: Digital Sticky Notes

When you spot a problem in a teammate's paragraph, you should not just erase it without warning. That causes anger and confusion. Instead, highlight the word and click Add Comment (💬). A comment sits in the right-hand margin like a polite sticky note!

2. The 3 Sentence Frames for High-Quality Feedback

When giving digital feedback, never leave rude, unhelpful notes like "This is bad" or "Wrong". Use these three professional sentence starters:

1
"I understood..." (Praise & Clarity)
Show your partner that their message was clear:
"I understood that dirty water causes cholera, which is a great point."
2
"One question I have is..." (Curiosity & Depth)
Ask for missing details politely:
"One question I have is: how much will the drum lids cost for our school?"
3
"One helpful suggestion is..." (Actionable Improvement)
Give a specific, realistic fix:
"One helpful suggestion is to make the 3 action steps into a bulleted list so it is easier to read."

3. Resolving Comments (Checking Off Tasks)

✔️ THE RESOLVE BUTTON
When a teammate leaves a suggestion in the margin and you make the recommended correction in the document, click the tiny checkmark icon (✓) at the top of the comment box. This resolves the comment and tucks it away cleanly!

4. Panic Button / Conflict Resolution

🚨 A TEAMMATE KEEPS DELETING MY WORK OVER AND OVER!
Stop typing immediately. Take your hands off the keyboard.
Do not start an "edit war" where you delete their work in revenge. Turn your chair, look your teammate in the eye, and discuss calmly: "Kwesi, why did you remove line 3? Let us agree on what belongs here." If you cannot agree, raise your hand and ask your Tech Club mentor to arbitrate!
