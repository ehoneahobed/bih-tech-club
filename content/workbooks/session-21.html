Session 21: Online Forms & Surveys

In Session 14 you filled in an online form. Today you swap sides: you will build one. You will make a real survey (a set of questions you ask many people) about your class, share it with a link or a QR code, collect your classmates' answers and watch them turn into charts. Next session, in Spreadsheets 3, you will analyse those answers like a real data scientist.

1. Plain Talk: The Class Prefect's Notebook

Imagine your class prefect must find out every pupil's favourite local food for the end-of-term party. She walks round with a notebook: "Kofi, what do you like? Esi? Yaw?" It takes the whole of break time. Then she counts the ticks, loses her place, and counts again. Two pupils were absent, so she has to chase them tomorrow.

An online form does the same job in a smarter way. The prefect types the question once and sends one link to the class WhatsApp group. Everyone answers on their own phone or computer, whenever they are free, and the form counts the answers and draws the chart by itself. Nobody loses their place.

Choosing the Right Question Type
Session 21 • Page 2

1. Seven Question Types

Every question in Google Forms has a type. You choose it from the drop-down box at the top right of the question. The right type makes the form easy to answer and easy to count.

Figure 21.2: What each question type looks like to the person answering.
💡 CHOICES ARE EASIER TO COUNT THAN TYPING
If ten pupils type their favourite food, you may get "jollof", "Jollof rice", "JOLLOF" and "jolof": four spellings the computer counts as four different foods! A multiple choice question with an Other option keeps the answers tidy. Use short answer or paragraph only when you really need people's own words.

2. Five Tools That Make a Form Work Well

1
Required: switch it on for questions everyone must answer. A red star (*) appears and the form will not send until it is answered. Leave "extra" questions optional.
2
Sections: click Add section to split a long form into pages ("About you", "Food & free time"). Shorter pages feel easier to finish.
3
Response validation: on a short answer question, click ⋮ → Response validation → Number → Between → 1 and 100. Now nobody can type "a lot" or 5000. Add a helpful error message, like "Please type a number from 1 to 100."
4
Settings tab → Responses: set Collect email addresses to Do not collect for a class survey. Limit to 1 response stops people answering twice, but everyone must then sign in with a Google account. Leave it off today, because classmates share Hub accounts.
5
Preview: click the eye icon and answer your own form as a stranger would. Is every question clear? Does the validation work? Fix it before anyone else sees it.
Share, Collect & Read the Results
Session 21 • Page 4

1. Asking Fairly: The Survey Maker's Promise

When people answer your survey, they trust you with information about themselves. A good survey maker keeps four promises:

✂️ ASK ONLY WHAT YOU NEED

For a party food survey you do not need names, phone numbers, home towns or birthdays. Every extra question is extra personal data to protect. Google already records the time of each answer, so you don't even need a date question.

📢 SAY WHY YOU ARE ASKING

Use the form description: "The Tech Club is asking about travel and food to practise data skills. Answers are anonymous and only our class will see the totals."

🔒 KEEP ANSWERS PRIVATE

Share the totals ("12 of us walk"), never one person's answers. Don't screenshot or forward someone's response.

🤝 NO TRICKS, NO PRESSURE

Answering is a choice. Write questions that don't push an answer: not "Don't you agree jollof is the best?" but "What is your favourite local food?"

2. Share by Link or QR Code

Click Publish, then Copy responder link (in older versions: Send → the link icon → Shorten URL → Copy). Paste the link into the class chat or an email. For a QR code, open the link in Chrome, click the ⋮ menu → Cast, save and share → Create QR code, and download it. Print it on a poster and people answer by pointing a phone camera at it.

3. Read the Results

1. Lab Mission: Build, Share and Collect a Real Survey

First, try the Survey Builder 3D lab on your session page: build a survey with different question types, share it, watch answers arrive and see them turn into a chart. Then, with your partner, build the real thing in Google Forms. Your answers will be the data for Session 22.

1
Start: sign in to the Hub learning account → forms.google.com → Blank form. Title: Our Class Survey (Pair __). Description: say who you are, why you are asking, and that answers are anonymous.
2
Section 1 "About you": (a) What class are you in? Drop-down: JHS 1, JHS 2, JHS 3. (b) How do you usually travel to school? Multiple choice: Walk, Trotro or bus, Taxi, Bicycle, Car, plus Add "Other". (c) How many minutes does it take you to get to school? Short answer with validation: number between 1 and 100. Make all three Required.
3
Add section 2 "Food & free time": (d) What is your favourite local food? Multiple choice: Jollof, Waakye, Fufu, Banku, Kenkey, Red-red, Other. (e) What do you do after school? Checkboxes: Homework, Help at home, Play sport, Watch TV, Read. (f) How many hours were you on a screen (phone, TV, computer) yesterday? Short answer, number between 0 and 24. (g) How much do you enjoy Tech Club? Linear scale 1–5. (h) One idea to make our school better: Paragraph, not required.
4
Settings and preview: Settings → Responses → email addresses: Do not collect. Preview (eye icon), answer it once yourself and test the validation by typing 500.
5
Survey swap: Publish, copy the link and give it to your mentor (or make a QR code). Answer at least 4 other pairs' surveys honestly. Then open Responses → Summary, and click Link to Sheets → Create a new spreadsheet.
