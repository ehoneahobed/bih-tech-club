Session 7: Email & Digital Communication

In Session 6, you learned how to browse websites and research facts across the World Wide Web. Today, we discover how to write and send messages directly to individuals, teachers, and organizations using Electronic Mail (Email).

1. Plain Talk: The Digital Post Office

Think about sending a physical letter through Ghana Post:

  • 1. You write your message on a sheet of paper.
  • 2. You place it inside an envelope and seal it.
  • 3. You write the recipient's exact P.O. Box address on the front.
  • 4. A postal van drives the envelope to their town over several days.

Email works the exact same way, but instead of paper and vans, the letter is converted into digital electronic signals that fly through cables across the world in less than three seconds!

2. Anatomy of an Email Address

Just like a phone number or P.O. Box, every person on email has a unique address. It is always divided into three clear parts by the "at" symbol (@):

Figure 7.0: Deconstructing an email address. Notice there are NEVER spaces anywhere in an email address!

3. The Golden Rule of Email Addresses

⚠️ ZERO SPACES ALLOWED!
An email address can never contain an empty space. If you type ama mensah@gmail.com, the computer will reject it or the message will bounce back as undelivered. Always use full stops or underscores to separate names: ama.mensah@gmail.com.

Netiquette & The 4-Part Email Structure
Session 7 • Page 3

1. What is "Netiquette"?

In Ghanaian culture, you would never walk up to an elder or teacher and blurt out a demand without first saying "Good morning, sir" or "Good afternoon, madam". Netiquette (Network Etiquette) is simply bringing our home training and respect into digital communication!

2. The Professional 4-Part Email Anatomy

Every formal email you write to a teacher, mentor, or school official must contain these four blocks:

1
The Respectful Salutation (Greeting):
Always address the person politely by their name and title:
"Dear Mr. Mensah," • "Good morning Madam Grace," • "Dear Club Mentor,"
2
The Courteous Opening:
A warm one-sentence greeting before diving into business:
"I hope this message finds you well." • "I hope you are having a productive week."
3
The Core Message (Body):
State your point clearly and briefly in 2 to 4 sentences. Use proper capital letters, full stops, and paragraphs.
4
The Respectful Sign-Off & Signature:
Close politely and include your full name and class so the teacher knows exactly who wrote to them:
"Respectfully yours," or "Best regards," → followed by: Ama Mensah, JHS 1 Student.

3. The Cardinal Rules of Netiquette

Rule Why It Matters What to Do
NO ALL-CAPS TYPING ENTIRE SENTENCES IN CAPITAL LETTERS IS SEEN AS SHOUTING AND ANGER. Use regular sentence capitalization.
Proofread Twice Once you hit Send, the email is gone! You cannot run after it or snatch it back. Check spelling and recipient address before clicking Send.
No Slang or Shortcuts Words like "u r gr8" or "wossop" belong in informal phone chats, never in school or professional emails. Write out full words: "you are", "please".
File Attachments & Inbox Safety
Session 7 • Page 4

1. What is an Attachment?

An Attachment is a separate computer file (such as a typed document, a drawing from Paint, or a PDF report) that you clip onto your email message, just like using a physical metal paperclip to fasten two papers together!

Figure 7.2: How attachments travel. The recipient receives your text message AND the attached document together!

2. Safe vs. Dangerous Attachments

✅ SAFE File Types (Normal school work)

• .docx or .txt (Word documents / text)

• .pdf (Readable electronic documents)

• .png or .jpg (Images and photos)

🚫 DANGEROUS Files (Never open from strangers!)

• .exe (Executable program - can be a virus!)

• .scr or .bat (Hidden programs and scripts)

• .zip from an unknown sender

3. Panic Button / Inbox Defense

🚨 RED ALERT: SUSPICIOUS EMAIL RECEIVED
If you receive an email from someone you do not know:
  • NEVER click on any links inside the message.
  • NEVER download or open any attachments.
  • NEVER reply with your password, phone number, or home address.
  • Raise your hand immediately and show your Tech Club mentor!
