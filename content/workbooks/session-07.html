Session 7: Peripherals & Connections

A peripheral is any device you connect to a computer to give it extra powers: a printer, a headset, a webcam, a projector. Today you will learn what each device does, which hole (port) it plugs into, how to connect without wires, and how to print without wasting paper.

1. Plain Talk: The Trotro Station

At a big trotro station like Kaneshie or Kejetia, every trotro has its own lane. The Madina bus waits in the Madina lane, the Kasoa bus in the Kasoa lane. Drive into the wrong lane and nobody gets anywhere. A computer is the same: every device needs the right port, and every port has its own shape so the wrong plug will not fit.

Figure 7.0: Like lanes at a trotro station, each kind of device has its own port. The shapes stop you using the wrong one.

2. Sort the Devices: Input, Output, Storage or Both?

Remember Session 1: input sends information into the computer and output brings it out to you. In Session 6 you met storage, which keeps files. Some devices do both input and output. Write each device in the right box.

2. What Plugs In Where?

PortWhat plugs in
USB-AMouse, keyboard, pen drive, printer cable, webcam, wired headset. The most common port of all.
USB-CNewer phones and laptops: charging, fast drives, even a monitor on some laptops.
HDMIMonitor, TV or projector. Carries picture and sound.
VGAOlder monitors and projectors (picture only). Still common in schools.
Audio jackHeadphones, speakers (green), microphone (pink). Laptops often have one jack for both.
EthernetA network cable to the router: steadier internet than Wi-Fi.
Charging / powerThe laptop charger or the desktop's power cable.
🚨 NEVER FORCE A PLUG
If a plug does not slide in gently, it is the wrong port or upside down. Look, turn it over and try again (remember the USB tongue from Session 5). Forcing it bends the pins and can break the port for good. Only plug in the power cable with dry hands, and ask an adult to handle wall sockets.
Wired vs Wireless: Bluetooth & Wi-Fi Devices
Session 7 • Page 3

1. Two Ways to Connect

🔌 WIRED

• Plug a cable into a port. Usually works straight away.

• Steady, no batteries needed, hard to lose.

• But you are tied to the computer by the cable.

📶 WIRELESS

• Bluetooth: connects one device to another over a short distance (about 10 metres): headsets, speakers, mice, phones.

• Wi-Fi: connects devices to a network, for example a printer the whole room can use.

• No cables, but needs charged batteries and setting up once.

2. Pairing a Bluetooth Headset (Windows 11)

Connecting two Bluetooth devices for the first time is called pairing. After that, they find each other by themselves.

1
Put the headset in pairing mode. Usually you hold its power or Bluetooth button until a light flashes (check its instructions).
2
On the PC: Start → Settings → Bluetooth & devices. Make sure Bluetooth is On.
3
Click Add device → Bluetooth. Wait for the headset's name to appear and click it.
4
When it says "Your device is ready to go", click Done. Play a sound to test it. To disconnect, switch the headset off or click it in Bluetooth & devices.
Figure 7.2: Pairing. The PC searches, the headset in pairing mode answers, and the two remember each other.

3. Wi-Fi Printers and Batteries

A Wi-Fi printer joins the same network as the computers, so everyone in the room can print to it without a cable. It appears in the print list like any other printer.

🔋 WIRELESS TROUBLE? CHECK THE BATTERY FIRST
A wireless mouse that stops moving or a headset that crackles usually has a flat battery. Charge it or swap the batteries before anything else. Switch wireless devices off when you finish, to save the battery.

Printing Well & Scanning
Session 7 • Page 4

1. The Print Dialog

In almost every programme, press Ctrl + P to print. A print dialog opens. It may look slightly different in Word, Chrome or Edge, but the choices are the same:

Figure 7.3: A typical print dialog. Check the preview and all five choices before you press Print.
📄 PRINT TO A FILE (PDF)

No printer? Choose Microsoft Print to PDF (in Word and most Windows apps) or Save as PDF (in Chrome and Edge). Click Print or Save, pick a folder and type a BIH file name. You get a PDF that looks exactly like the printed page.

🌳 SAVE PAPER AND INK

• Always check the preview first.
• Print only the pages you need.
• Choose black and white unless colour matters.
• Use two-sided printing for long documents.
• Press Print once. Pressing again prints it twice!

2. Scanning: Paper Into the Computer

A scanner turns a paper page into a picture or PDF file. Lift the lid, lay the page face down, close it, then use the scanner's app (or the Windows Scan app) to choose PDF or JPG and click Scan. No scanner? Many phones can scan documents with the camera app or with the Scan button in the Google Drive app. Lay the page flat in good light, keep the phone straight above it, and crop to the edges.

2. Lab Mission: Three Stations

Your mentor will move you round the stations. Tick each step when it works.

A
Sound station: plug a headset or speakers into the audio jack (or a USB port, or pair by Bluetooth). Click the speaker icon on the taskbar, then the arrow > next to the volume slider and choose your device. (Or Settings → System → Sound → Output.) Play a short sound and set a comfortable volume.
B
Projector station: connect the projector's HDMI or VGA cable to the PC and switch the projector on. Press Windows + P and try each mode: PC screen only (projector blank), Duplicate (same on both), Extend (one big desktop across both), Second screen only (only the projector). End on Duplicate.
C
Print station: open the file your mentor gives you, press Ctrl + P and choose: page 1 only, 1 copy, black and white. Print it on the real printer if your mentor says so, or choose Microsoft Print to PDF and save it in your BIH_Practice folder as Print_Test_YourName.
