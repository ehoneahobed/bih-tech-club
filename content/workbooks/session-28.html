Session 28: Photos, Audio & Video

Phones and computers have turned everybody into a photographer, a radio presenter and a film-maker. But a blurry, dark photo or a shaky video with loud wind noise tells nobody anything. Today you will learn to take a good photo, edit it, make it small enough to send, record and trim a voice note, and make a 30-second video about your community with your team.

1. Plain Talk: Media Is Like Cooking Jollof

Good jollof needs three stages. First you buy good ingredients: fresh tomatoes, not rotten ones. Then you prepare: wash, cut away the bad parts, cook. Finally you serve the right portion on the right plate. Nobody serves a whole pot to one person!

Media works the same way. You capture good raw material (a sharp, bright photo or clear sound). You edit it: crop, trim and cut away the bad parts. Then you export it at the right size for where it is going: small for WhatsApp, bigger for printing. And before you serve anybody's face to the world, you ask first.

Taking a Good Photo
Session 28 • Page 2

1. The Four Habits of a Good Photographer

☀️ 1. LIGHT

Light should fall on your subject, not come from behind it. Stand with the window or the sun behind you. A person standing in front of a bright window becomes a dark shadow.

🤲 2. STEADY

Hold the phone with two hands, elbows tucked in, or lean on a wall. Tap the screen where you want it sharp. Wipe the lens with a soft cloth first.

🔲 3. RULE OF THIRDS

Imagine two lines across and two lines down. Put your subject on a line or where lines cross, not always in the dead centre. Most cameras can show these grid lines (look for "Grid" in the camera settings).

🙋🏾 4. ASK FIRST

Before you photograph a person, ask: "May I take your photo? It is for my Tech Club project." If they say no, that is the end of it. For your projects, photograph places, objects and hands instead of faces.

Edit Photos: Crop, Rotate, Resize, Compress
Session 28 • Page 3

1. Four Words Every Editor Knows

WordWhat it doesMarket example
CropCuts away the edges you don't want. The picture shows less.Cutting the stranger's elbow out of your photo of the stall.
RotateTurns the picture so it stands up straight.Fixing a photo that came out sideways.
ResizeMakes the whole picture fewer pixels wide and high. It shows the same thing, just smaller.Shrinking a big phone photo so it fits a web page.
CompressSqueezes the file so it takes fewer megabytes, by throwing away detail your eyes hardly notice.Making 20 photos small enough to send on a small data bundle.

2. Editing in the Windows Photos App

1
Double-click the photo in File Explorer; it opens in Photos. Click Edit image (the pencil icon, or Ctrl+E).
2
Crop: drag the white corners inwards. Choose an aspect ratio (shape) such as Square or 16:9 if you need one. Rotate: use the rotate button or the straighten slider.
3
Click Save options → Save as copy, so the original stays safe. (A real editor never throws away the raw ingredients.)
4
Resize and compress: in Photos, click … (See more) → Resize image. Choose a smaller size and a lower quality, then save as a new file. Menus may look slightly different on your version.

Record Audio & Make a Short Video
Session 28 • Page 4

1. Record and Trim a Voice Note

On a Windows 11 computer, open Sound Recorder from the Start menu (on a phone, use the voice recorder app). Plug in a headset with a microphone if you have one. Find a quiet corner, hold the microphone a hand's width from your mouth, press the red Record button, speak, and press Stop. Recordings are usually saved in Documents → Sound recordings.

Trimming means cutting off the silent or messy bits at the start and end ("Erm… is it on?"). Many phone recorders have a Trim or Edit button. Windows Sound Recorder cannot trim, so on the computer drag the recording into Clipchamp and cut it there.

2. Storyboard First, Film Second

A storyboard is a comic-strip plan of your video: one box per shot, with a quick sketch, what is said and how many seconds it lasts. Film-makers in Kumasi's Kumawood plan this way, and so will you. Six shots of 5 seconds each make 30 seconds.

Figure 28.3: The timeline stacks titles, video and music on separate tracks. Drag the edge of a clip to trim it.

3. Music You Are Allowed to Use

Popular songs (Afrobeats hits, gospel, highlife) are protected by copyright, just like the pictures in Session 27. Use the free music inside your video editor's library, or tracks marked free to use. Videos with someone else's song may be muted or taken down.

🛑 CONSENT AND PRIVACY: THE VIDEO RULES
• No faces of other people without their permission (and a parent's permission for children).
• No school uniforms, crests or name tags in anything that may go online (the School Uniform Rule: in Session 33 you will see why it matters).
• No house numbers, car number plates or signs that show exactly where someone lives.
• Film places, hands, objects and the backs of heads instead. They tell the story just as well.
