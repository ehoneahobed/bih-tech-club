Session 2: Keyboard Mastery & First Typing

In Session 1, you learned how to start up your computer safely and guide the pointer using the mouse or touchpad. Today, we conquer the Keyboard—the tool you use to write stories, calculate numbers, and give commands to the machine.

1. Ghanaian Wisdom: "Tikro nkɔ agyina" (One head does not hold council)

Our elders teach that one person alone cannot do all the thinking; we need each other. Typing is the same: beginners often "chicken peck" with a single index finger, searching for letters slowly. Today, you will learn to rest all 10 fingers on the keys so they share the work and typing becomes fast, relaxed, and natural.

2. Desktop Keyboard vs. Laptop Keyboard

🖥️ Desktop Keyboard (Full-Sized)

Has wide spacing between keys. On the far right side, it includes a separate Numeric Keypad (a block of number keys like a calculator). Connects with a USB wire.

💻 Laptop Keyboard (Compact)

Built directly into the laptop chassis. To save space, the number keys run in a single row across the top, and it includes a special Fn (Function) key to adjust screen brightness and volume.

1. Keys That Control Your Writing

Before typing words, you must know what the special control keys do:

Key on Board What Happens When You Press It Everyday Life Analogy
Spacebar
(Long bar at bottom)		 Inserts one empty space between words. Tap once with either thumb. Like lifting your pencil to leave a gap between words on paper.
Enter ↵
(Large key on right)		 Drops your cursor down to start a brand-new line. Also confirms an action. Like moving down to the next blue line in your exercise notebook.
Backspace ←
(Top-right of main block)		 Erases the character directly to the LEFT of the blinking cursor. Like an eraser wiping out the last letter you just wrote.
Delete
(Near navigation arrows)		 Erases the character directly to the RIGHT of the blinking cursor. Like pulling forward the next letter and wiping it out.
Caps Lock
(Left side middle)		 Locks typing into ALL CAPITAL LETTERS. An indicator light turns on. Like turning on a megaphone: EVERYTHING BECOMES BIG!
Shift ↑
(Left and Right sides)		 Hold down Shift while tapping a letter to make just one capital letter. Like writing a capital letter at the start of a sentence or name.

1. Finding Home Row Without Looking Down

Close your eyes. Put your index fingers on the keyboard and slide them across the middle row. Do you feel the tiny raised plastic bumps?
• Your Left Index Finger rests on the bump on key F.
• Your Right Index Finger rests on the bump on key J.
These bumps are your anchors. Every other finger lines up naturally beside them!

Figure 2.2: Home Row Finger Positions. Each finger has its own home key. Curved fingers, wrists off the desk!

2. Posture & Wrist Health

💡 TYPING LIKE A PIANIST
Curve your fingers gently like you are holding a small egg in each palm. Do not rest your wrists flat against the desk while typing! Keep your wrists floating slightly so your hands can move freely without strain.

Capital Letters & Punctuation

1. Making Capital Letters: Shift vs. Caps Lock

When to Use the SHIFT Key (95% of the time)

Use Shift when you need only one capital letter (like the first letter of a name or sentence):

  1. Hold down Shift with your left or right pinky finger.
  2. Tap the letter key once with the other hand.
  3. Release the Shift key immediately.

Example: Holding Shift and tapping k types capital K.

When to Use the CAPS LOCK Key

Use Caps Lock only when you need to type an entire paragraph or acronym in capitals (e.g., GHANA or UNESCO):

  1. Tap Caps Lock once. A small green/amber light turns on.
  2. Type your words (they will all be capitals).
  3. Remember to tap Caps Lock again to turn the light off!

3. Panic Button / Troubleshooting

🚨 PROBLEM: Everything I type is screaming in ALL CAPS!
Look down at your keyboard. Find the Caps Lock key on the left. There is a tiny glowing LED light on the key or near the top right of the keyboard. Tap Caps Lock once to turn off that light. Now type again—your lowercase letters are back!
💡 PROBLEM: As I type new letters, my existing words are disappearing!
In some programs (for example Word with overtype turned on, or LibreOffice), the Insert (or Ins) key switches on Overtype mode. Tap Insert once to switch back to normal typing.

1. Launching Your Writing Tool: Notepad

Follow these steps to open the computer's simplest digital writing pad:

1
Click Start: Move your pointer to the bottom-left corner of the screen and click the Start button once.
2
Search: Without clicking anything else, type the word notepad on your keyboard. A search box will automatically open and find the program.
3
Open: Press the Enter key once. A blank white window with a blinking vertical line (cursor) will appear!

3. Double-Click & Drag Practice

Practice these two essential pointer techniques inside your Notepad document:

🖱️ Double-Click to Select a Word

Move your mouse (or glide your touchpad finger) until the I-beam cursor touches the word Ghana that you typed.
Quickly tap twice: Click-Click!
Observation: The entire word highlights in blue. When text is highlighted, any new key you tap replaces it immediately.

🖱️ Click-and-Drag to Highlight

Place your pointer just before the letter W in Welcome.
Click and HOLD the left button down. While holding it, slide your pointer to the right over the word. Let go.
Observation: You have manually highlighted a selection of text!
