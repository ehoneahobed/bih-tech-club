Session 10: Spreadsheets, Tables & Basic Formulas

In Level 4, you learned how to use a word processor to write essays and notices. Today, we unlock a completely different digital tool designed to handle numbers, lists, money, and calculations: the Spreadsheet!

1. Plain Talk: The Market Trader's Ledger Book

If you visit a busy shop or stall at Makola Market or Kejetia Market, the shopkeeper keeps a thick paper ledger book ruled into rows and columns. They write down items sold, quantities, and prices. At the end of the day, they use a pen or calculator to add up the cash totals line by line.

An electronic Spreadsheet (like Microsoft Excel, Google Sheets, or LibreOffice Calc) is an intelligent digital ledger. When you change a single price or quantity, the spreadsheet recalculates all totals automatically in less than a millisecond!

Figure 10.0: The Spreadsheet Grid. Columns are Letters, Rows are Numbers, and Cells are their intersection.

2. Essential Spreadsheet Vocabulary

  • Column: A vertical strip of boxes identified by a Letter (A, B, C...).
  • Row: A horizontal line of boxes identified by a Number (1, 2, 3...).
  • Cell: A single box in the grid formed where a Column meets a Row (e.g., Cell B2).
  • Active Cell: The box currently selected with a dark green border around it.
  • Formula Bar (fx): The long white box at the top where formulas and raw data are displayed.

1. Cell Coordinate Addresses (Column Letter + Row Number)

Every single box in a spreadsheet has its own unique home address. You find the address by reading the Column Letter first, then the Row Number second:

Figure 10.1: Reading cell coordinates. Column C meets Row 3 at Cell C3.

2. The Two Basic Types of Data

🔤 TEXT (Labels)

Words, names, descriptions, or titles. Text helps people understand what the numbers mean.

Examples: Tomatoes, Ama Mensah, Exercise Books

Alignment: The computer automatically hugs text to the LEFT side of the cell, so it reads naturally, like a book.

🔢 NUMBERS (Values)

Raw digits, quantities, prices, percentages, and measurements that can be mathematically calculated.

Examples: 10, 25.50, 100

Alignment: The computer automatically pushes numbers to the RIGHT side of the cell, so units, tens and hundreds sit under each other for easy adding.

The Golden Law of Formulas
Session 10 • Page 3

1. What is a Formula?

A Formula is an electronic mathematical instruction you give to a cell. Instead of doing the math in your head and typing the answer, you write a formula that commands the computer to calculate it for you!

👑 THE GOLDEN LAW OF SPREADSHEETS:
EVERY FORMULA MUST START WITH AN EQUALS SIGN: =
If you forget to type the = sign first, the computer thinks you are just typing plain words and will NOT calculate anything!

2. The Four Computer Math Operators

Computers do not use the math symbols from your primary school exercise notebook. Study these keyboard operators:

Math Operation Old Paper Symbol Computer Keyboard Key How to Write It in a Cell
Addition + + (Shift + =) =B2 + C2
Subtraction − - (Minus key) =B2 - C2
Multiplication × * (Asterisk: Shift + 8) =B2 * C2   (Never type the letter 'x'!)
Division ÷ / (Forward slash) =B2 / C2

3. Why We Use Cell Addresses (Not Fixed Numbers!)

❌ BAD FORMULA: =10 * 8

If you type =10 * 8, the cell shows 80. But if the store manager changes the price to 9 Cedis tomorrow, your total stays stuck at 80! It is dead math.

✅ SMART FORMULA: =B2 * C2

You tell the cell: "Multiply whatever number is in B2 by whatever is in C2!" If prices change next week, the total updates automatically! This is called Dynamic Calculation.

The AutoFill Magic Handle & Error Fixing
Session 10 • Page 4

1. What is the AutoFill Handle?

Imagine having a sales list of 200 items in a school store. You do not have to type =B3*C3, =B4*C4, =B5*C5 two hundred times!

When you select a cell, look at its bottom-right corner. There is a tiny dark green square called the AutoFill Handle. When you drag it down, the computer copies the math formula and updates the row numbers automatically!

Figure 10.2: The AutoFill Handle. When the cursor turns into a thin black cross (+), drag down to copy formulas.

2. Panic Button: Spreadsheet Error Messages Decoded

Error on Screen What Actually Happened How to Fix It in 2 Seconds
#####
(Train Tracks)		 The number is too wide to fit inside the cell column width! Move your pointer to the line between column letters (e.g. between D and E) and double-click to widen it!
#NAME? You misspelt a function or name (e.g. =SUMM(B2:B5)). Click the cell and fix the spelling in the Formula Bar. (Forgot the =? Then the formula just shows as plain text.)
#VALUE! You tried to do math with words (e.g. multiplying "Books" * 10). Check your formula: make sure it points only to cells with numbers inside.
