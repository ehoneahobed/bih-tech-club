Session 30: Calendars, Video Calls & Cloud Storage

Clubs, schools, churches, mosques and businesses all need to answer the same three questions: When do we meet? Where do we meet? Where are our files? Today you will learn the three online tools people use to answer them, and at the end you will plan a whole team meeting online, from the invitation to the shared folder.

Think of the noticeboard outside the Hub. It shows the meeting dates, and someone has to remember to look at it. The meeting itself happens in a room you must walk to. And the club's files sit in a cupboard you can only open when you are in the building.

Digital Calendars & Invitations
Session 30 • Page 2

1. Create an Event with a Reminder

The two most common calendars are Google Calendar (calendar.google.com) and Outlook Calendar (in Outlook or outlook.com). Both work in the same way. An event is anything with a date and time: a meeting, a match, a birthday.

1
Click Create (Google) or New event (Outlook), or simply click an empty time on the calendar grid.
2
Type a clear title ("Tech Club planning meeting"), set the date, start and end time, and add a location or a video meeting link.
3
Add a reminder (Google calls it a notification), for example "1 day before" and "30 minutes before".
4
To invite people, type their email addresses under Add guests (Google) or Invite attendees (Outlook), then Save and Send.
Figure 30.1: An invitation and the three replies. Always reply, so the organiser knows who is coming.

A shared club calendar is one calendar that several people can see (and some can edit). The club secretary adds every meeting, match and holiday once, and everyone sees it on their own phone or computer. In Google Calendar, a new calendar is made under Other calendars → +; your mentor will share the club one with you.

Video Meetings & Meeting Manners
Session 30 • Page 3

1. Joining a Video Meeting

The best-known video meeting tools are Google Meet, Zoom and Microsoft Teams. You can join in two ways:

🔗 BY LINK

Click the link in the calendar event or the message (for example meet.google.com/abc-defg-hij). Check your name, microphone and camera, then click Join now or Ask to join.

🔢 BY CODE

Open the app or website and type the meeting code (Meet), or the meeting ID and passcode (Zoom and Teams). The organiser gives you these.

Figure 30.2: The control bar of a video meeting. Find these six buttons in any app.
📶 VIDEO EATS DATA
On a phone, an hour-long video call can use hundreds of megabytes, sometimes over 1 GB, of your data bundle. Use Wi-Fi when you can. Turning your camera off (and not sharing your screen) saves most of the data, and the call often works better on a weak signal.

Cloud Storage: The Shared Cupboard
Session 30 • Page 4

1. What Is "the Cloud"?

Cloud storage means keeping your files on a company's big computers (called servers) instead of only on your own PC. You reach them through the internet, from any computer or phone, by signing in. The two you will meet most are Google Drive (drive.google.com) and Microsoft OneDrive (onedrive.com, and built into Windows 11).

Figure 30.3: Sync. One file in the cloud; every device you sign in on sees the newest copy.

2. The Five Cloud Jobs

JobHow (Google Drive; OneDrive is almost the same)
FolderNew → New folder. Name it with the BIH naming standard, e.g. TechClub_Meeting_Team3.
UploadNew → File upload, or drag a file from File Explorer into the browser window.
DownloadRight-click the file → Download. A copy lands in your Downloads folder.
SyncIn Windows 11, OneDrive appears in File Explorer. A cloud icon means "online only"; a green tick means "also on this PC"; arrows mean "syncing now".
ShareYou learned this in Session 29: Viewer, Commenter or Editor, and share with named people, not "anyone with the link".

3. Storage Limits

Free cloud space is limited. A free Google account gets 15 GB, shared between Gmail, Drive and Photos. A free Microsoft account gets 5 GB of OneDrive. (School and work accounts can be different.) Remember Session 6: 1 GB is about 1,000 MB.

⚠️ CLOUD SAFETY ON SHARED COMPUTERS
On a club or school PC, always sign out of Drive or OneDrive when you finish, or the next person can open your files. Don't tick "Stay signed in" on a shared computer. The cloud is a cupboard, so lock it!

2. Lab Mission: The Whole Meeting, End to End

Your team is planning the Tech Club Open Day. One pupil is the organiser; then swap so everyone has a turn.

1
Shared folder: in Drive (or OneDrive), make a folder TechClub_OpenDay_TeamN. Upload one file from today (for example your Session 27 poster). Share the folder with your teammates' Hub learning accounts as Editor.
2
Calendar event: create "Open Day planning meeting" for a day next week, 30 minutes long. Add a Google Meet (or Teams) video link, a reminder 30 minutes before, and paste the folder link into the description.
3
Invite: add your teammates and your mentor as guests and send. Each teammate opens the invitation and replies Yes, Maybe or No.
4
Practice call: join the meeting from the link (on headphones, to avoid a loud squeal of feedback). Each person: unmute and say hello, mute again, raise a hand, send a chat message. The organiser shares their screen and shows the folder.
5
Tidy up: everyone leaves the call, then signs out of the account.
