Session 18: What Is AI? Machines That Learn

You hear about it on the radio, on TV and from older brothers and sisters: AI. Some people say it will do everything; some people are afraid of it. Today you will find out what AI really is, how it learns, and why it sometimes gets things badly wrong. No magic, just ideas you can understand.

1. Plain Talk: What Is Artificial Intelligence?

Artificial Intelligence (AI) is a computer program that has learned to spot patterns from a very large number of examples, so it can make a good guess about something new.

Think about how you learned to tell a ripe plantain from an unripe one. Nobody gave you a rule book. Your grandmother simply showed you plantain after plantain: "This one is ripe. This one is not ready." After many examples, you could look at a plantain you had never seen before and guess correctly. AI learns in a similar way: from examples, not from a list of rules.

💡 AI IS NOT ALIVE
AI does not think, feel or understand like a person. It has no opinions and does not "know" things the way you do. It is a very powerful pattern-guessing machine, built and controlled by people.

2. Rules or Examples? Two Ways to Make a Computer Smart

In Sessions 16 and 17 you wrote programs in Scratch. You gave the computer exact rules: "IF touching sachet THEN change score by 1." AI works differently: people give it examples and it works out the pattern by itself.

AI Is Already All Around You

1. Three Jobs AI Can Do

Most AI you will meet does one of three jobs:

👁️ 1. RECOGNISE

It works out what something is: a face, a voice, a word in a photo, a sick cassava leaf.

🔮 2. PREDICT

It guesses what comes next: the next word you will type, the next video you might like, tomorrow's rain.

✨ 3. CREATE

It makes something new from patterns it learned: a paragraph, a picture, a song, a voice. This is called generative AI.

When AI Gets It Wrong: Mistakes & Bias
Session 18 • Page 4

1. AI Always Guesses, and Guesses Can Be Wrong

AI never knows an answer for certain. It makes its best guess from the patterns it learned. Sometimes the guess is wrong, and the worrying part is that AI can sound just as confident when it is wrong as when it is right.

2. Garbage In, Garbage Out

Computer scientists have a saying: "Garbage in, garbage out." If the training data is wrong, too small or one-sided, the AI learns the wrong pattern:

Figure 18.2: Garbage in, garbage out. Bad training data teaches the AI bad patterns.

3. Bias: When AI Is Unfair

When one-sided data makes an AI treat some people worse than others, we call it bias. In 2018, a researcher named Joy Buolamwini, whose family comes from Ghana, tested face-analysis programs from big technology companies. They made far more mistakes on darker-skinned women than on lighter-skinned men, largely because the photos used to train and test them were mostly of lighter-skinned faces. Her work pushed companies to improve their systems.

🧑🏾‍⚖️ PEOPLE MUST STAY IN CHARGE
AI can help doctors, teachers, farmers and banks, but a person should check its important decisions. AI should be a helper, never the final judge of someone's exam, health or future.
