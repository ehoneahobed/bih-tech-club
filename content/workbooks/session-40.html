Session 40: Using AI Wisely & Safely

In Session 39 you learned that AI is a pattern-guessing machine that learns from examples. Today you meet the kind of AI everyone is talking about: chatbots that write answers to your questions. You will learn how to ask them good questions, how to catch their mistakes, and how to stay safe and honest when you use them.

1. Plain Talk: What Is an AI Chatbot?

An AI chatbot (for example ChatGPT, Gemini, Copilot or Meta AI in WhatsApp) is a program you can "chat" with by typing or speaking. It was trained on a huge amount of text from books and websites. From all that text it learned which words usually follow which other words.

When you ask a question, the chatbot predicts a good-sounding answer one word at a time, a bit like your phone keyboard suggesting the next word, but far more powerful.

Figure 40.0: A chatbot predicts likely words. It picks words that sound right, which is not always the same as true.
⚠️ AGE RULES AND SUPERVISION
Most AI chatbots say users must be at least 13 years old, and under-18s need a parent's or guardian's permission. In the BIH Tech Club, you only use a real AI tool with your mentor, at the Hub, or with a parent or guardian beside you.
Talking to AI: The C.L.E.A.R. Prompt
Session 40 • Page 2

1. What Is a Prompt?

A prompt is the instruction or question you type to an AI. A vague prompt gets a vague answer. A clear prompt gets a useful one. It is like sending a younger cousin to the market: "Buy food" brings back anything, but "Buy 6 eggs and a tin of tomatoes, under 50 cedis" brings back what you need.

2. The C.L.E.A.R. Prompt Formula

Letter What it means Example
C CONTEXT: who you are and who it is for "I am a JHS 1 pupil in Ghana…"
L LIMITS: how long and in what shape "…in 5 short bullet points…"
E EXAMPLES & DETAILS: the facts it needs "…with examples from farming and electricity…"
A ASK: one clear task "Explain how the Volta River helps Ghana."
R REVIEW: read the answer and check it Is it true? Is it what I asked? Ask a follow-up if not.
❌ Vague prompt

Tell me about rivers.

Which rivers? For whom? How long? The AI has to guess.

✅ C.L.E.A.R. prompt

I am a JHS 1 pupil in Ghana. Explain how the Volta River helps Ghana, with examples from farming and electricity, in 5 short bullet points.

AI Can Be Wrong: Check Before You Trust
Session 40 • Page 3

1. Confident but Wrong: "Hallucinations"

Because a chatbot predicts words that sound right, it sometimes makes things up: wrong dates, invented quotes, books that do not exist, even fake website links. People call this a hallucination. The answer often looks neat, polite and very sure of itself, so mistakes are easy to miss.

2. The 3 Checks

1
Compare with a trusted source: your textbook, your teacher or mentor, or an official website (for example one ending in .gov.gh or .edu.gh).
2
Does it make sense? If something sounds strange, too perfect or surprising, slow down and check it twice.
3
Find a second source: one website or one chatbot is not enough. Two trusted sources that agree are much safer.

Deepfakes, Privacy & Honesty
Session 40 • Page 4

1. Deepfakes: When Seeing Is Not Believing

AI can now create photos, videos and voices that look and sound real. A fake one made with AI is called a deepfake. Scammers have used cloned voices to phone people: "Mum, it's me! I'm in trouble, send MoMo now!"

🚩 YOUR SESSION 33 S.T.O.P. SKILLS STILL WORK
Deepfake scams use the same tricks as phishing: panic, urgency and requests for money or PINs. Stop, then check another way: hang up and call the person back on the number you already know, or ask a family question only the real person could answer. Never share a shocking video or voice note until you know it is real.

2. Privacy: What You Type May Be Kept

Messages you type into an AI tool can be stored by the company and may even be used to train future AI. Treat a chatbot like a public noticeboard: never type your P.I.I. (full name with your school, home address, phone or MoMo number, passwords or PINs), and never upload photos of other people without their permission.

3. Honesty: Helper, Not Cheater

AI can be a patient tutor: it can explain a hard idea in a new way or quiz you before a test. But if AI does your homework and you hand it in as your own, you learn nothing, and it is cheating. Follow your school's rules, and if you used AI, say so.

How is the AI being used? Helper ✅ Cheater ❌
Asking AI to explain fractions with a market example, then doing the homework yourself.
Copying an AI-written essay and putting your name on it.
Asking AI for 5 quiz questions to test yourself before an exam.
Using AI to answer test questions while the teacher is not looking.
