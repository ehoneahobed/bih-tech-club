Session 12: Presentations & Slide Design (Part 1)

In Levels 4 and 5, you learned how to type documents in a Word Processor and calculate data in a Spreadsheet. But what if you have a great idea and you need to convince your school assembly, your teachers, or your community elders to take action? You need a Digital Presentation!

1. Plain Talk: The Speaker & The Signboard

Imagine the senior school prefect speaking at morning assembly. If they hold up a giant cardboard sign with an entire textbook page written in tiny handwriting, no student can read it. But if their sign has a bold 3-word title and one clear drawing, everyone understands in a flash!

Presentation software (like Microsoft PowerPoint, Google Slides, or LibreOffice Impress) lets you create digital signboards called Slides. The slides project behind you on a screen or TV to support your voice.

Figure 12.0: Anatomy of presentation software. Slides run down the left, and the active slide is built in the center.

2. Essential Presentation Vocabulary

  • Slide: A single digital page or screen in your presentation.
  • Slide Deck: The complete collection of all slides saved together in one file.
  • Placeholder: The dotted rectangular boxes on a slide that say "Click to add title" or "Click to add text".
  • Speaker Notes: Private written reminders at the bottom of the screen that only you can see on your laptop.
The 6x6 Design Law & Contrast
Session 12 • Page 2

1. The Cardinal Presentation Law

👑 THE SPEAKER'S GOLDEN RULE:
SLIDES ARE MEANT TO SUPPORT YOU, NOT REPLACE YOU!
If you type every single word on your slide and stand with your back to the audience reading it out loud like a robot, your audience will fall asleep. Your slides should show keywords and pictures; your voice tells the story!

2. The Famous "6-by-6" Rule

Follow this simple rule to keep your slides clean, readable, and punchy:

1. Maximum 6 Bullets

Never put more than 6 bullet points on a single slide. If you have 8 points, split them into two slides!

2. Maximum 6 Words

Keep each bullet point to a maximum of 6 words. Use strong keywords instead of long, full sentences.

Figure 12.1: The 6x6 rule in action. Short phrases let the audience listen to the speaker.

3. High Contrast: Colors That Work

✅ EXCELLENT Color Combinations ❌ TERRIBLE Combinations (Unreadable!)
Deep Navy or Black text on a Pure White / Cream slide. Yellow text on a White background (invisible!).
Crisp White text on a Deep Purple or Dark Blue slide. Red text on a Green background (blurs human eyes).
Slide Layouts & Aspect Ratio
Session 12 • Page 3

1. Standard Slide Layouts

When you click "New Slide" on the ribbon, the software asks you which layout you need:

  • Title Slide: Used for Slide 1 only. Contains a giant Title box and a Subtitle box.
  • Title and Content: The workhorse layout. Has a title at the top and a large bulleted box below.
  • Two Content: Divides the slide into two columns (great for text on the left and a picture on the right!).

Using Google Slides or LibreOffice Impress? The buttons look a little different but do the same job.

2. The Aspect Ratio Law: Never Squish a Picture!

When you click a picture, eight small sizing dots (handles) appear around its border. How you drag them matters enormously:

Figure 12.2: Resizing images. Always drag from the CORNER handles to preserve the natural proportions.

3. Panic Button / Troubleshooting

🚨 MY PICTURE LOOKS SQUISHED LIKE A SMASHED PLANTAIN!
Don't panic! You accidentally dragged a middle-side handle instead of a corner handle.
How to fix it: Immediately press Ctrl + Z to undo the resize. Then, find one of the four circular corner handles, click, and drag diagonally!

1. Why Professionals Plan on Paper First

The greatest mistake beginners make is opening presentation software immediately and getting distracted by fancy animations and colors. Expert presenters always plan on a sheet of paper first using a Storyboard (a sequence of quick hand-drawn sketch boxes).

Guided Hands-On Lab: Your First Deck
Session 12 • Page 5

1. Lab Mission: Create a 3-Slide Campaign Deck

Follow these 6 steps to build your first digital presentation in PowerPoint, Google Slides, or Impress:

1
Open Software & Choose Theme:
Click Start → type powerpoint (or open Google Slides / Impress) → Choose a clean, simple theme with a white or light background.
2
Build Slide 1 (Title Slide):
• Click in the Title placeholder box: type SAFE WATER FOR OUR SCHOOL.
• Click in the Subtitle box: type Community Project by BIH Tech Club → press Enter → type your name and class.
3
Add Slide 2 (The Challenge):
Click the New Slide button on the ribbon → select Title and Content.
• Title: The Water Challenges We Face
• Content box (3 short bullets):
  • Uncovered outdoor storage drums
  • Mosquito breeding in stagnant water
  • Waterborne illnesses cause missed classes
4
Add Slide 3 (The Action Steps):
Click New Slide → select Title and Content.
• Title: Our 3-Step Action Plan
• Content box:
  • Cover all drums with tight lids
  • Clean and scrub tanks weekly
  • Install clean ceramic pot filters
5
Insert & Resize an Image:
On Slide 3, click Insert → Pictures → This Device → BIH_Practice > Images (your mentor puts pictures there), or use Insert → Shapes. Add a water drop or bucket.
Resize it using only the CORNER handles.
6
Run the Slide Show & Save:
Press the F5 key on your keyboard. Your slides fill the whole screen!
Tap the Spacebar to advance through your slides. Tap Esc to exit.
Save: File → Save As → Documents > BIH_Practice → Name: Safe_Water_AmaMensah.pptx.
