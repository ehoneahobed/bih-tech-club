Session 5: File Operations & Typing Fluency

In Session 4, you built your first folder and saved your student profile inside. Today, you will learn the superpower tools used by computer experts around the world: Copy, Cut, Paste, Rename, and how to safely manage the Recycle Bin and USB Flash Drives.

1. Everyday Analogy: Photocopy Machine vs. Moving a Desk

📋 COPY & PASTE (The Photocopy Machine)

When you photocopy a syllabus sheet, the original paper stays in your hand, and a brand-new identical twin appears. You now have TWO copies in two different places!

Shortcut: Ctrl + C → then → Ctrl + V

✂️ CUT & PASTE (Moving a Desk)

When you pick up a wooden desk from JHS 1 and carry it into JHS 2, you did not create a second desk. You simply moved the original. It is gone from JHS 1!

Shortcut: Ctrl + X → then → Ctrl + V

2. Visual Comparison: Copy vs. Cut

Figure 5.0: The difference between Copying and Cutting. Notice how 'X' in Ctrl+X looks like a pair of scissors!

3. Two Quick Shortcuts: F2 (Rename) and Ctrl + Z (Undo)

💡 PRO TIPS: RENAME AND UNDO
Rename: click the file once, then press the F2 key at the very top of your keyboard. The name highlights blue, so type the new name and press Enter!
Undo: made a mistake? Press Ctrl + Z straight away to reverse your last action.
Deleting Files & The Recycle Bin
Session 5 • Page 2

1. What Happens When You Delete a File?

When you delete a file on your computer, it does not vanish into thin air immediately. The computer safely transfers it to a special temporary holding basket on your Desktop called the Recycle Bin.

Think of it just like the wastepaper basket in your classroom. If you accidentally throw your homework sheet into the wastepaper basket, you can easily reach in, pull it out, and smooth it flat again!

Figure 5.1: The Recycle Bin safety net. You can always Restore a deleted file unless the bin has been emptied, the file was on a USB pen drive (those are deleted straight away), or you used Shift + Delete.

2. How to Recover an Accidentally Deleted File

1
Open the Recycle Bin: Go to your Desktop screen and double-click the Recycle Bin icon.
2
Find Your File: Look through the list for the file you accidentally deleted.
3
Click "Restore": Right-click on the file name. Click Restore in the pop-up menu. The file magically jumps straight back into its original folder!

Portable Storage: USB Flash Drives
Session 5 • Page 3

1. What is a USB Flash Drive (Pen Drive)?

A USB Flash Drive (commonly called a Pen Drive in Ghana) is a thumb-sized storage device. Because it does not need internet or cables, students and teachers use it to carry school files from one computer to another.

Figure 5.2: Gentle USB insertion. Align the hollow side with the internal plastic tongue of the port.

2. The Cardinal Rule: "Safely Remove Hardware" (Eject)

🚨 NEVER YANK OUT A PEN DRIVE WHILE IN USE!
If you pull a flash drive out while the computer is saving, the files on the drive can be permanently corrupted and destroyed! Always follow this ejection routine:
  1. Look at the bottom-right corner of your screen (the System Tray).
  2. Click the tiny upward arrow ^ to reveal hidden icons.
  3. Click the small picture of a USB drive with a checkmark, then click "Eject" next to your drive's name.
  4. Wait until the screen says "Safe To Remove Hardware" before pulling it out!
Typing Fluency: The Top Row Reach
Session 5 • Page 4

1. Expanding Beyond Home Row: Reaching the Vowels

Your fingers always rest anchored on the Home Row (A S D F and J K L ;). But the most common vowels in the English language—E, I, O, U—live on the Top Row!

To type a top-row letter: reach up with the assigned finger, strike the key once, and immediately snap the finger back home!

Figure 5.3: Reaching up from the Home Row anchors to the Top Row vowels and letters. Always snap fingers back home!

2. The Gold Standard: Accuracy Over Speed

🏆 WHY SLOW AND ACCURATE WINS
Never rush to type fast! If you type 40 words a minute with 10 mistakes, you will waste time erasing and fixing typos. A calm student who types 20 words a minute with zero errors often finishes first!

Follow these 5 practical challenges inside your BIH_Practice folder:

2
Make a Duplicate (Copy & Paste):
Click your profile file once. Press Ctrl + C (Copy).
Then press Ctrl + V (Paste).
Observation: A duplicate named Profile_AmaMensah - Copy.txt appears!
3
Rename the Duplicate:
Click the copy file once. Press the F2 key at the top of your keyboard.
Type: Profile_Backup and press Enter. (Windows keeps the .txt ending for you.)
4
Move It (Cut & Paste):
Click Profile_Backup.txt. Press Ctrl + X (Cut). Notice how the icon fades slightly.
Double-click your Notes subfolder to open it.
Press Ctrl + V (Paste). The backup file is now safely inside Notes!
5
Delete & Restore Drill:
Create a scrap file: Right-click → New → Text Document → Name it TrashMe.
Select it and tap the Delete key. It vanishes!
Go to the Desktop, open the Recycle Bin, find TrashMe.txt, right-click it, and click Restore. Check your folder—it is back!
