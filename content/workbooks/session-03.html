Session 3: The Desktop and Programmes

When you sit down at your school desk, you place your exercise book, pen, ruler, and eraser in front of you. In the computer world, your screen is called the Desktop because it acts as your electronic work table!

1. Anatomy of the Desktop Workspace

Look at your screen right now. Every desktop screen has four main zones:

Figure 3.0: The Desktop Screen. The Taskbar sits on the bottom rim, keeping track of every open program.

2. Plain Talk: The Four Core Elements

  • 1. The Wallpaper: The picture or color covering your screen surface.
  • 2. Desktop Icons: Small labeled pictures that act as shortcuts to open programs, folders, or files.
  • 3. The Taskbar: The long bar at the bottom. It shows the Start button and small icons for every program currently running.
  • 4. The System Tray: Found on the bottom-right corner. It displays background information: sound volume, internet connection, the time, and the battery level (if using a laptop).
What is a Programme (Application)?
Session 3 • Page 2

1. Everyday Analogy: Different Tools for Different Work

To cut wood, you pick up a saw. To knock in a nail, you pick up a hammer. You would never cut timber with a hammer!

In computing, a Programme (also called an Application or App) is a specialized electronic tool built to do one particular job:

Programme Type What It Lets You Do Examples on Your Computer
Word Processor Write letters and essays, then format and print them. Microsoft Word, LibreOffice Writer, Google Docs.
Text Editor Type plain notes with no styling. Notepad.
Drawing / Paint Tool Draw shapes, diagrams and posters. MS Paint, TuxPaint.
Web Browser Read, search and watch lessons on the internet. Google Chrome, Microsoft Edge, Mozilla Firefox.
Calculator Add, subtract, multiply and divide quickly. Windows Calculator app.

2. Two Ways to Open (Launch) Any Programme

Method 1: From a Desktop Icon

1. Glide your pointer over the icon on the desktop screen.

2. Perform a fast Double-Click (tap the Left Button twice fast: Click-Click!).

3. Wait 2 seconds. The program opens inside a box called a Window.

Method 2: Using the Start Menu Search

1. Left-click the Start Button in the bottom-left corner.

2. Immediately type the program name (e.g., paint or calculator).

3. Tap the Enter key on your keyboard.

3. Icons Decoded: Can You Spot the Difference?

Look at the small pictures on your desktop. They fall into three groups:

  • Program Icon: The tool itself (e.g., a paintbrush for Paint, a blue 'W' for Word). Often has a tiny curved arrow in the corner showing it is a shortcut.
  • File Icon: A single document you or someone else created (e.g., a sheet of paper with writing).
  • Folder Icon: A yellow plastic-looking folder used to organize multiple files together.

Window Anatomy & Window Controls
Session 3 • Page 3

1. What is a "Window"?

When you launch an application, it appears inside a framed rectangular area called a Window (this is why the operating system is named Microsoft Windows!). A window lets you see and work inside one program at a time.

Figure 3.1: The Window Anatomy. The three magic control buttons always sit in the top-right corner.

2. Master the Three Magic Window Buttons

Button Symbol Button Name What Actually Happens When You Click It
[ − ] Minimise The window instantly disappears from view, but it is NOT closed! It tucks down into the bottom taskbar, waiting for you.
[ □ ] Maximise / Restore Grows the window to cover the entire screen. Once maximised, the icon changes to two overlapping squares (Restore Down) to shrink it back.
[ × ] Close Shuts the application down completely. If you have unsaved drawings or words, the computer will ask you if you want to save first!

3. Moving and Resizing a Window

How to MOVE a Window

1. Move your pointer over the empty gray space on the Title Bar at the very top.

2. Click and HOLD the left mouse button (or hold the bottom-left touchpad corner).

3. Slide your mouse across the desk. The whole window slides with you! Release to park it.

How to RESIZE a Window

1. Move your pointer very slowly to the outer rim or corner of the window frame.

2. Watch the arrow pointer transform into a double-headed arrow (↔ or ↕).

3. Click and drag inward to shrink the window, or drag outward to make it bigger!

Multitasking: Juggling Two Programmes
Session 3 • Page 4

1. What is Multitasking?

In school, you might have your Science textbook open on your left while writing notes in your exercise notebook on your right. A computer can do this too! Having more than one program running at the same time is called Multitasking.

Figure 3.2: Overlapping windows. The "Active" window has the colored title bar; the "Inactive" window sleeps behind it.

2. Three Ways to Switch Between Open Windows

  • Method 1 (The Taskbar Click): Move your pointer down to the bottom taskbar. Click on the icon of the program you want. It immediately pops to the front!
  • Method 2 (The Background Tap): If any small edge of the background window is peeking out, click on it once. It instantly leaps to the front.
  • Method 3 (The Pro Keyboard Shortcut): Hold down the Alt key with your left thumb, and tap the Tab key once. A small selector box appears. Release both keys to swap windows!

3. Panic Button / Troubleshooting

🚨 HELP! I clicked the minus button [-] and my whole document vanished! Did I delete my work?
No, your work is 100% safe! You clicked the Minimise button. Minimise never erases anything; it simply rolls the window up and parks it on the bottom shelf (the Taskbar).

How to get it back: Look at the bottom taskbar rim. Find the icon with a tiny glowing dot or blue line underneath it. Click that icon once. Your window will instantly leap back onto the screen!

2. Closing Properly: The "Save Changes?" Dialog Box

When you click the red [ X ] button to close a program, the computer protects you from accidentally throwing your work into the dustbin:

Figure 3.3: The standard Save Warning dialog box. Always read before clicking!
Button Option What the Computer Does When You Click It
Save Stores your work so you can open it next week.
Don't Save Closes the program and permanently throws away everything you typed/drew today. (Use only for scrap practice).
Cancel "Oops, I didn't mean to close!" Takes you back to your document.

On Windows 11, Notepad may close without asking and keep your text for the next person. Close the tab (Ctrl + W) and choose Don't save.
