Session 32: Smartphones & Tablets

In most Ghanaian homes, the first "computer" anybody owns is a phone. Your mother pays for electricity with it, your uncle checks the football scores on it, and your grandmother talks to family abroad on it. Today you will learn to use a smartphone or tablet like an expert: change its settings, install apps safely, save data, share internet, find your way with maps and keep the family's Mobile Money safe.

1. Plain Talk: A Whole Market in Your Pocket

A smartphone is a small computer that can also make calls. A tablet is the same thing with a bigger screen (and often no SIM card for calls). Both have a processor, memory and storage, just like the computers you met in Session 6.

Think of a busy market like Makola or Kejetia. At the entrance you can see the stalls; you walk to the one you need (the tailor, the fish seller, the phone repairer). A smartphone works the same way. The home screen is the market entrance, and every app is a stall that does one job: a torch, a calculator, a radio, a camera, a map, even a bank counter.

💡 MOST PHONES IN GHANA RUN ANDROID
In Session 8 you met operating systems. Most phones here (Samsung, Tecno, Infinix, itel, Nokia and others) run Android, made by Google. iPhones and iPads run iOS and iPadOS, made by Apple. This session teaches Android, with notes on how iPhones differ. Every brand looks slightly different, but the ideas are the same.

1. Quick Settings: The Light Switches

Swipe down from the top of the screen twice to open Quick Settings. Each tile is like a light switch: tap it to turn something on (the tile lights up) or off. Press and hold a tile to jump to its full settings.

Figure 32.1: Quick Settings. Blue tiles are on; dark tiles are off. Tap the pencil (Edit) button to choose which tiles appear.

🔒 ALWAYS USE A SCREEN LOCK
A phone with no lock is like a house with the door wide open: anyone who picks it up can read the messages and open the MoMo app. Use a PIN of at least 6 digits or a fingerprint, never 1234, 0000 or your birthday, and don't let friends watch you unlock it.

Installing Apps Safely & Freeing Storage
Session 32 • Page 3

1. Before You Press Install: Developer, Downloads, Reviews, Data safety

Only install apps from the official store: the Google Play Store on Android, or the App Store on iPhone. Fake apps copy the name and logo of popular apps to steal money or data, so do these 4 checks before you tap Install:

1
Developer: under the app's name. Is it the real company? (The real WhatsApp is from WhatsApp LLC, not "WhatsApp Free Gold Edition".)
2
Downloads and rating: a popular app has many downloads and a good star rating. A copy may have very few.
3
Reviews: read some of the newest reviews, not just the stars. "It stole my data" is a red flag.
4
Data safety: scroll to the Data safety section to see what the app collects and shares. Does it need all that?
⚠️ NEVER INSTALL APPS FROM A WHATSAPP LINK
Messages like "Download this app to get free data!" often send an APK file: an Android app from outside the Play Store. Android warns you before it will install one. Such apps can hide viruses or steal MoMo details. If you see a warning, press Cancel and tell an adult.

2. App Permissions: Who Gets the Keys?

A permission is your "yes" or "no" when an app asks to use part of your phone, such as the camera, microphone, location or contacts. Android lets you choose:

Figure 32.2: A permission request. If the reason doesn't make sense, choose Don't allow. You can change it later in Settings → Apps → (the app) → Permissions.

3. "Storage Full!" How to Free Space

🧹 Clear out

• Uninstall apps you never use.
• Delete WhatsApp videos and voice notes you don't need (WhatsApp → Settings → Storage and data → Manage storage).
• Empty the photo app's bin.

☁️ Back up first

Photos can be backed up to the cloud (for example in Google Photos), as in Session 30. Back up on Wi-Fi, check they are safe, then free space on the phone.

Data Bundles, Hotspots & MoMo Safety
Session 32 • Page 4

1. Where Does My Data Bundle Go?

Mobile data is internet from the phone network, paid for with a data bundle. Wi-Fi is internet from a router (at home, school or the Hub) and does not use your bundle. Some things use far more data than others:

Uses a LOT of data 🔴Uses some data 🟠Uses very little 🟢
Watching videos (YouTube, TikTok): an hour can use 1 GB or more; video calls (WhatsApp, Meet)Voice calls on WhatsAppText messages on WhatsApp
App and phone updates; backing up photos and videosScrolling social media with picturesChecking email (without big attachments)
💡 SIX WAYS TO MAKE A BUNDLE LAST
1. Use Wi-Fi when you can. 2. Turn on Data Saver (Settings → Network & internet → Data Saver; Samsung: Connections → Data usage → Data saver). Turn Data Saver off before you switch on a hotspot. 3. Set the Play Store to update apps over Wi-Fi only. 4. Back up photos on Wi-Fi only. 5. Watch videos at a lower quality. 6. Turn mobile data off when you are not using it.

2. Sharing Internet with a Hotspot

A hotspot turns a phone into a little Wi-Fi router, so a laptop can use the phone's data. Find it in Quick Settings, or Settings → Network & internet → Hotspot & tethering (Samsung: Connections → Mobile Hotspot and Tethering).

Figure 32.3: A hotspot. The laptop joins the phone's Wi-Fi, but every megabyte comes from the phone's data bundle.

Hotspot rules: set a strong hotspot password (never leave it open), share it only with people you know, watch the bundle (a laptop can download big updates quickly) and turn the hotspot off when you finish, because it also drains the battery.

3. Mobile Money: Protect the Family's Wallet

📩 A typical scam message

From: 0551234567
"You have received GHS 500.00 from Kwame A. New balance GHS 512.40." Then a call: "Sorry, I sent it to you by mistake. Please send it back quickly!"

✅ What a smart family does

The real message comes from the network's official name, not an ordinary number. Check the balance yourself in the network's official MoMo menu or app. If no money came in, it is a scam: send nothing.

🛑 THE GOLDEN MOMO RULES
1. Never share the MoMo PIN with anyone, not even someone who says they work for the network. The network will never call or text to ask for it.
2. "You have won a prize! Send a fee to claim it" is always a scam.
3. Never approve a payment request you did not start.
4. If something feels wrong, stop, tell a parent, and call the network's official customer service number to report it.

📍 SHARING YOUR LOCATION
Google Maps can share where you are, live (tap your profile picture → Location sharing). Only ever share it with your own family, and only with a parent's agreement. Never share your live location with a stranger or in a public post.
