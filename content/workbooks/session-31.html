Session 31: Learning Online

Many senior high schools, universities and training courses now give out homework, notes and feedback online. Some of your future teachers will say: "It's on Google Classroom." Today you get ready for that. You will join an online class, hand in work, read feedback, find good tutorials, take neat digital notes and build study habits that beat distraction.

1. Plain Talk: The Classroom That Lives in Your Pocket

Think about your real classroom. There is a noticeboard where the teacher pins announcements. The teacher writes tasks on the board with a due date. You put your exercise book in the pile on the teacher's table. Later, it comes back with red-pen comments and a mark. And you can go to the teacher's desk to ask a quiet question.

Google Classroom is all of that on a website and a phone app:

Figure 31.0: The parts of Google Classroom, and the real classroom they copy.
🎓 WHY THIS MATTERS FOR SHS
Pupils who already know how to find a task, hand in on time and read feedback online start senior high school a step ahead. Other schools may use Microsoft Teams or Moodle instead: the ideas are the same.
Using Google Classroom
Session 31 • Page 2

1. Join a Class with a Code

1
Go to classroom.google.com (or open the Classroom app) and sign in with the account your school or the Hub gave you.
2
Click + (top right) → Join class. Type the class code your teacher gives you: a short mix of letters and numbers. Click Join.
3
Sometimes the teacher invites you instead: then you just click Join on the class card.

2. Find, Do and Hand In an Assignment

4
Open the Classwork tab, or the To-do list, which shows every task from every class. Click the assignment, read the instructions and the due date.
5
Under Your work, click + Add or create. Attach a file from Google Drive, a link, or a file from your computer, or create a new Doc.
6
Click Hand in (it says Turn in on American-English settings), then confirm. Made a mistake? Click Unsubmit, fix it and hand in again, ideally before the due date.
Figure 31.1: The journey of an assignment. Your job is not finished until you read the feedback.

3. Comments: Class or Private?

👥 CLASS COMMENT

Everyone in the class can read it. Use it for questions that help everybody: "Is the diagram on page 4 or 5?"

🔒 PRIVATE COMMENT

Only you and the teacher can read it. Use it for your own work: "I attached the wrong file, sorry. The right one is now attached."

Finding & Judging Good Tutorials
Session 31 • Page 3

1. Where to Find Tutorials

A tutorial is a lesson that shows you how to do something, step by step. Good free places to start:

WhereWhat it is good for
Khan AcademyFree maths, science and computing lessons with practice questions (khanacademy.org and its app). It runs as a non-profit and has no adverts.
YouTubeMillions of video lessons, from excellent teachers to people who are wrong. You must judge each one (see below).
Your teacher's linksLinks in Google Classroom are chosen to match your syllabus. Start there.

2. Judge It Before You Trust It: The 4 Checks

Remember the 4-Way Shield from Session 12 (Who? When? Why? Second source?)? For tutorials we swap "Why?" for "Match?": Who? When? Match? Second source?

3. Learning with AI, Responsibly

AI chatbots can explain a topic in simpler words or quiz you before a test. In Session 40 you will learn to use them properly. For now, remember three rules: use AI to help you understand, not to do your homework for you; check what it says with a second source, because AI can be confidently wrong; and be honest with your teacher about how you used it. Only use AI tools with a mentor or parent.

Digital Notes & Study Habits
Session 31 • Page 4

1. Take Digital Notes That You Can Actually Use

Good notes are not a copy of everything the teacher said. They are short, organised and easy to find again. Use Google Docs for longer notes, Google Keep for quick notes and checklists, or Microsoft OneNote, which works like a notebook with sections and pages.

Figure 31.3: A good digital note. Use the Heading styles you learned in Session 17 so the headings show in the outline.

2. Study Habits with Tech

HabitHow
Focus timerWork for 25 minutes, then take a 5-minute break. Windows 11's Clock app has Focus sessions that time this for you.
Silence the phoneTurn on Do Not Disturb (or Focus mode) and put the phone face down, or in another room.
One tab at a timeClose the tabs you don't need. "Just one quick video" can eat an hour.
Rest your bodyEvery 20 minutes, look at something far away for 20 seconds. Drink water. Stretch.

3. Learning Without the Internet

Data is expensive and the network is not always there. Plan ahead: download the lessons you need while you are on Wi-Fi at the Hub (some apps, such as YouTube in many countries, have a Download button for watching later), save notes on your device, and borrow from the club's offline kit: a USB drive of lessons, videos and practice files your mentor keeps.
