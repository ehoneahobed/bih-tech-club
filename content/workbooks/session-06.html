Session 6: Searching the Web & Internet Foundations

Until now, your computer has worked completely alone inside our classroom. But when you connect your computer to the Internet, it joins a worldwide conversation linking millions of schools, libraries, scientists, and communities across every continent!

1. Plain Talk: The Road Network of Computers

Think about the road network in Ghana: untarred feeder roads connect small villages to highways, which lead to major cities like Accra, Kumasi, and Tamale. Vehicles travel along these roads carrying goods and letters.

The Internet is an electronic road network: millions of computers connected by underground copper lines, radio cell towers, and giant undersea glass cables laid deep under the Atlantic Ocean that come ashore right here on the coast of Ghana (in Accra and Tema)!

Figure 6.0: How your school computer connects across the Atlantic Ocean to reach global knowledge servers.

2. Internet vs. World Wide Web: Don't Confuse Them!

The Internet (The Physical Road) The World Wide Web (The Cars on the Road)
The massive worldwide network of physical computers, wires, routers, and radio signals. The collection of multimedia pages, documents, pictures, and videos that travel across that network.
Analogy: The train tracks and train stations. Analogy: The passenger train carrying passengers and books.
The Web Browser (Your Digital Vehicle)
Session 6 • Page 2

1. What is a Web Browser?

To visit a website, you need a specialized program called a Web Browser. The browser translates code written across the world into beautiful, readable pictures and text on your screen.

Common browsers you will see on Ghanaian computers include: Google Chrome, Microsoft Edge, Mozilla Firefox, and Apple Safari.

Figure 6.1: Key controls of a modern Web Browser. Always notice the green Security Padlock on the left!

2. Browser Navigation Controls Decoded

Control Button What It Does When Should You Click It?
Back Arrow (←) Returns to the previous page you were reading. When you opened a result and want to go back to the search list.
Forward Arrow (→) Moves forward to the page you just backed out of. When you hit Back by mistake!
Refresh (↻) Reloads the page from scratch across the network. When a page loads slowly, shows an error, or freezes.
New Tab (+) Opens a fresh, clean browsing page in the same window. When you want to search something new without losing your current page.
Web Addresses: URLs Decoded
Session 6 • Page 3

1. What is a URL?

Just as every home or school in Ghana has a physical location or GhanaPost GPS address (e.g., GA-183-9024), every single page on the World Wide Web has its own unique digital address called a URL (Uniform Resource Locator).

Figure 6.2: Anatomy of a Web Address (URL). The extension tells you who runs the site!

2. Domain Extensions: Who Runs the Website?

The last few letters of a web address tell you what kind of organization created it:

Extension What Kind of Organization Reliability for School Homework
.edu / .edu.gh Universities, Polytechnics, and Colleges. HIGH: Verified academic professors & researchers.
.gov / .gov.gh Official Government Ministries & Departments. HIGH: Official laws, public statistics, and curriculum.
.org Non-profit charities, clubs, and global bodies. GOOD: Educational (e.g. Wikipedia, UNESCO, Red Cross).
.com Commercial business company. CAUTION: May be trying to sell you products or show ads!

3. The Security Padlock: HTTPS vs. HTTP

🔒 THE MEANING OF THE PADLOCK
When you look at the address bar, check if there is a tiny closed padlock icon and the letters https:// (the 's' stands for Secure). This means information passing between your computer and that website is scrambled into secret code so thieves and eavesdroppers cannot steal it.
Search Engines & Smart Keywords
Session 6 • Page 4

1. Browser vs. Search Engine: Don't Confuse Them!

🚗 THE WEB BROWSER (The Car)

The application software on your computer (Chrome, Edge, Firefox). It is the vehicle that drives you across the internet.

📚 THE SEARCH ENGINE (The Head Librarian)

A website living inside your browser (Google, Bing, DuckDuckGo) that keeps an enormous index of billions of pages to help you find answers.

2. The Art of Keyword Searching

Search engines do not need conversational chat words like "please" or "tell me". They hunt for Key Target Words:

❌ Weak Search (Too vague or full of filler) ✅ Smart Keyword Search (Specific & Direct)
trees in ghana (Millions of random results) mangrove tree species coastal protection Ghana
how can our school get some clean drinking water? ceramic pot water filtration schools Ghana
plastic plastic sachet recycling projects Accra

4. Panic Button: Deceptive Pop-Ups & Flashing Traps

🚨 WARNING: "YOU HAVE WON A FREE IPHONE & 5,000 CEDIS!"
While browsing, you might see flashing boxes claiming you won a lottery or prize.
Rule: If you did not buy a ticket, you did not win! Never click these boxes. Never enter your name, school, or phone number. Immediately click the tiny [ X ] on the tab to close it!
