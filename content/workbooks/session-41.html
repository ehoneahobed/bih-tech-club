Station 1: The Mango Catch Game
Session 41 • Page 2

⏱ Time limit: 25 minutes  ·  🧰 You need: Scratch (scratch.mit.edu or the Scratch app). Use any sprites from Scratch's library, for example the Apple (our mango) and the Bowl.

1. Your Brief

🎮 GAME BRIEF: MANGO CATCH
A mango falls from the top of the tree. The player moves a basket left and right with the arrow keys. Every time the basket catches the mango, the Score goes up by 1 and a new mango falls from the top. If the basket misses, the mango goes back to the top and falls again. When the green flag is clicked, the score must start at 0 and the basket goes to the bottom of the stage. Save it as Scratch_MangoCatch_[YourName].sb3.
🧩 New blocks you may need
when [right arrow] key pressed (Events): runs the blocks under it each time that key is pressed.   change x by / change y by (Motion): moves the sprite a little sideways (x) or up and down (y); a minus number moves left or down.   pick random -200 to 200 (Operators): gives a different number each time, so the mango falls from a new place.   touching [Bowl]? (Sensing): true when the sprite touches the Bowl.   y position (Motion): how high the sprite is now.   < (Operators): "less than", e.g. (y position) < (-170) means "lower than -170", the bottom of the stage.

Station 3: Robot Trainer Challenge
Session 41 • Page 4
⏱ Time limit

10 minutes

🧰 You need

1. Your Brief

🤖 THE CHALLENGE
Press Forget everything so the robot starts with no training data. Train it until it sorts 5 out of 5 correctly into Plastic, Paper and Cans. Use as few examples as you can. Then explain, like an AI scientist, why it worked.

2. Re-try Hints

1
Teach at least 2 examples of every kind: 2 Plastic, 2 Paper and 2 Cans. One-sided data makes the robot unfair to the other kinds (Session 39).
2
Check every label before you teach it. One wrong label is garbage in, garbage out.
3
If it gets one wrong, look at which example it thought was most similar. Teach one more example that looks like the item it got wrong, then test again.
