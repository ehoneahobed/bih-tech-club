# Session 7: Email & Digital Communication

> **At a glance:** pupils read an email address, fill in To, Cc, Bcc and Subject correctly, write a polite 4-part email with a safe attachment, and know what to do with a suspicious message. · **Time:** 90 minutes (adjust the timings if your period is shorter) · **Workbook pages:** 6

## Learning outcomes
By the end of the session, pupils can:
1. Name the three parts of an email address (username, @, domain) and explain why it can never contain a space.
2. Use To, Cc, Bcc and Subject correctly and write a clear 4–7 word subject line.
3. Write a formal email using the 4-part structure and the rules of netiquette.
4. Tell safe attachment types (.docx, .pdf, .jpg) from dangerous ones (.exe, .scr, .bat, unknown .zip) and report a suspicious email instead of opening it.

## Before the session
- [ ] Decide how pupils will "send": the page 5 simulator form in the workbook (default) or a supervised school email account. Do not ask pupils to create personal accounts in the session.
- [ ] Write a real email address that you control on the board for the page 5 lab. The workbook no longer gives one, and the example addresses in it (such as example.org) are made up.
- [ ] If using real accounts, check they work and can send to that address.
- [ ] Put a small, safe file such as Water_Project_Draft.docx on each PC or USB so pupils can practise attaching.
- [ ] Load Internet Town and Spot the Phish on the projector PC and test both.

## Key ideas and how to explain them
- **The digital post office:** write the letter, seal the envelope, write the P.O. Box address, the van delivers. Email does the same in under three seconds.
- **Email address anatomy:** username, the @ sign, the mail provider's domain. Zero spaces, ever: use a full stop or underscore (ama.mensah@gmail.com).
- **To vs Cc:** To is the person who must read and reply; Cc (carbon copy) is for people who just need to know, like your mentor when you email the headteacher. Bcc (blind carbon copy) is a hidden copy: other recipients cannot see who was Bcc'd. Use it when emailing many people who do not know each other, so their addresses stay private.
- **Subject line:** a 4–7 word headline. "hi" looks like spam, "URGENT HELP ME!!!!" creates panic, blank looks like an accident.
- **Netiquette:** bringing home training into digital messages. Greeting, courteous opening, 2–4 sentence body, polite sign-off with full name and class.
- **Attachments:** a digital paperclip. Normal schoolwork is .docx, .pdf, .png, .jpg; never open .exe, .scr, .bat or a .zip from a stranger.

## The 3D lab
In **Internet Town**, demonstrate **Send an email to Kofi with your teacher in Cc** and ask the class to count the envelopes that arrive at the friend's mail server: one for To, one for Cc. Then switch to **Spot the Phish**. Demonstrate **Read the first email and decide: Safe or Phishing?**, pointing at the sender's full address, not just the name. Pairs then work through **Catch 3 phishing emails and shred them** and **Sort all 8 emails**, reading the explanation after each answer. This prepares pupils for the inbox scenarios on page 6.

## Common mistakes and quick fixes
| Pupils often… | Try this |
|---|---|
| Put a space in the address (ama mensah@…) | Point to the Golden Rule box: no spaces, use a full stop. |
| Type the @ sign wrongly or cannot find it | Shift + 2 on most keyboards; on some UK-layout keyboards it is Shift + the apostrophe key. |
| Put the mentor in To and the headteacher in Cc | Ask "Who must reply?" That person goes in To. |
| Leave the subject blank or write "hi" | Formula: topic, class, name, as in "Capstone Project Idea - JHS 1A - Ama Mensah". |
| Forget to attach the file they mention | Make "Is the paperclip there?" part of the pre-flight check. |

## When things go wrong
- **No internet:** use the simulator form on page 5 (it needs no connection) and the 3D labs from the offline pack.
- **Not enough PCs:** pairs: one types, one reads the pre-flight checklist aloud. Each pupil must still write their own subject line.
- **A PC freezes or the power goes:** most email services save drafts, but tell pupils not to rely on it. The workbook page is the backup; pupils finish on paper.

## Answer key
**1. Subject Line Makeover Challenge.** Look for: 4–7 words that say what the email is about, with no ALL CAPS, no "!!!!" and, ideally, a name or class. Examples:
- hello sir: "Science Project Inquiry - Kofi Mensah" (given in the workbook).
- URGENT OPEN THIS NOW: for example "Request for Help - Coding Challenge 3".
- homework: for example "Maths Homework - JHS 1B - Ama Mensah".

**2. Safe vs Suspicious Inbox Scenarios**
1. Email from headteacher@school.edu.gh, "End of Term Examination Timetable.pdf": **Safe to Open**. It comes from a known school address and the file is a .pdf.
2. Email from winner-claim99@lottery-prize.xyz with claim_money.exe: **Report to Mentor**. Prize you never entered, strange address and an .exe file.
3. "Your account will be deleted in 10 minutes unless you click and type your password": **Report to Mentor**. Panic deadline plus a request for a password.

**Pre-flight Send Checklist:** every box should be ticked truthfully before sending.

## Support and stretch
- **Needs more support:** give them the page 5 simulator text to copy, then ask them to change just the subject line and one body sentence.
- **Finished early:** write a second email to the headteacher with the mentor in Cc, or aim for **Score 7 or more out of 8** in Spot the Phish.

## Evidence for sign-off
The pupil shows a finished email (sent, simulated or on paper) with a correctly spelt recipient, a clear 4–7 word subject, all four parts of the structure, a safe attachment type, and correct answers to the three inbox scenarios.

## Safety and wellbeing
Pupils use only the fictional addresses in the workbook, the address you write on the board, or supervised school accounts; never personal accounts or real passwords. Remind them: never click links or open attachments from strangers, never reply with a password, phone number or home address, and raise a hand to show you. Thank pupils who report something, even if it turns out safe. If real accounts are used, check every pupil signs out before leaving a shared PC.
