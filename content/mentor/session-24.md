# Session 24: Presentation Delivery & Public Speaking

> **At a glance:** pupils polish their Safe Water deck with calm transitions, animations and a source citation, then deliver it live to a partner using keyboard controls and confident stage presence. · **Time:** 90 minutes (adjust the timings if your period is shorter) · **Workbook pages:** 6

## Learning outcomes
By the end of the session, pupils can:
1. Explain the difference between a transition (the stage curtain) and an animation (the actor on stage), and follow the no-carnival rule.
2. Control a live show with F5, Spacebar or →, ←, B and Esc.
3. Deliver a 90-second talk with good pace, volume and 3-zone eye contact, cite a source, and answer a question using the 3-step method.

## Key ideas and how to explain them
- **Transition = stage curtain:** it moves the whole slide (Fade, Push, Wipe). **Animation = actor on stage:** it moves one item inside a slide (Appear, Fade, Fly In).
- **No-carnival rule:** no spinning, bouncing or screeching sounds. Use Fade or Appear, and only to stop the audience reading ahead.
- **Steering keys:** F5 launches, Spacebar or → goes forward, ← goes back, B blacks the screen, Esc exits.
- **The B key:** all eyes on you for a story or a plea; press B again to bring the slide back.
- **Three pillars:** pace (not an express tro-tro; pause 2 seconds after each main point), volume (speak from your chest for the back row), eye contact (left row, centre row, right row).
- **Citing sources:** giving credit builds trust; a small 8 pt grey line at the bottom is enough. Only cite a source pupils actually looked up. The Figure 24.3 numbers are made up, and its footer says so; that honesty is part of the lesson.
- **3-step question method:** listen without interrupting, say "Thank you, that is a great question" to buy thinking time, answer in 1 or 2 sentences or honestly say you will research it.

## The 3D lab
Use **Slide Design & Show Time**. On the projector, demonstrate *Choose a calm animation (None or Fade), not the carnival!* and *Start the slide show*. In pairs, pupils do *Press B during the show to black the screen, then B again* and *Move through every slide to the end with → and hear the applause*. The lab is a safe rehearsal for steps 2, 3 and 5 of the workbook lab.

## Common mistakes and quick fixes
| Pupils often… | Try this |
|---|---|
| Choose a spinning or bouncing effect | Point to the no-carnival rule; switch to Fade or Appear. |
| Apply the transition to only one slide | Click "Apply to All" on the Transitions tab. |
| Make the citation big and bold | Set it to 8 pt and grey, at the very bottom of Slide 2. |
| Cite a famous agency for numbers they never checked | Help them find the real source, or label the numbers "Sample data (made up for practice)". |
| Turn round and read from the projection | Stand beside the screen, face the class, glance at the laptop only. |
| Press F5 in Google Slides and lose the page | Use the Slideshow button instead. |
| Rush the talk in 30 seconds | Use the rehearsal timer: 20, 40, 30 seconds. Make them pause and breathe. |

## When things go wrong
- **No internet:** PowerPoint and Impress work offline. Google Slides users present from an offline PC or use your spare deck.
- **Not enough PCs:** groups of three: one presents, one evaluates with the rubric, one runs the keyboard on cue. Rotate.
- **A PC freezes or the power goes:** save after each lab step. If the power goes, carry on with the rehearsal without slides: pupils present from their paper storyboards and still practise pace, volume and eye contact.

## Answer key
**Check Your Knowledge (Page 2):** the **← (left arrow)** key. Backspace or Page Up also go back in most programs, but the workbook teaches ←.

**Peer Evaluation Rubric (Page 6):** open task. Look for: ticks that match what you saw; praise that names something specific ("You looked at all three rows"); a tip that is kind and doable ("Pause after each bullet"). Return any rude comments for rewriting.

**Lab check (Page 5):** Fade on all slides; Slide 3 bullets appear one per click; an 8 pt grey citation on Slide 2 that names a real source the pupil looked up or says "Sample data (made up for practice)"; talk of about 90 seconds (20 + 40 + 30) with B used on Slide 2.

## Support and stretch
- **Needs more support:** let nervous pupils present to you or to one friend first, seated if needed. Let them read the slide titles as prompts. Focus on one pillar only, such as volume.
- **Finished early:** add speaker notes for each slide and present using them. Act as the "teacher" asking a question so the presenter practises the 3-step method, or try the ← key to revisit a point on request.

## Evidence for sign-off
The pupil shows their saved deck with a calm transition on all slides, click-by-click bullets on Slide 3 and a small citation on Slide 2. They deliver the talk with F5 or the Slideshow button, use B and Esc correctly, face the audience, and have a completed peer rubric from their partner. They can explain transition versus animation in their own words.

## Safety and wellbeing
Public speaking can be frightening; never force a pupil to present to the whole class. Keep peer feedback kind, specific and private to the pair. Pupils should only photograph or film a classmate presenting with that classmate's permission. Remind pupils to work on their own deck only and to ask before deleting or replacing anyone else's file.
